A Key to Reading the Bible

What This Key Is

This Key is a simple tool for reading Scripture with love, truth, humility, and freedom.

It is not another authority telling people what they must believe.

It is a way to ask better questions, examine interpretations, compare Scripture with Scripture, and remain willing to discover that we may be wrong.

The Key is first a mirror, not a weapon.

The Foundation: The Key Is First a Mirror

Before asking, “What is wrong with them?” we can ask:

“What is this showing me, and what does it reveal about me?”

This does not mean blaming ourselves for someone else’s harmful behavior.

It means taking responsibility for our own choices, responses, boundaries, words, and hearts.

Their choice belongs to them. My choice belongs to me.

We can recognize harm, establish boundaries, disagree, and speak truthfully without condemning another person’s worth.

Intention Test: Why Am I Using the Key?

Before applying the Key, examine the intention behind the search.

Am I seeking truth, or trying to prove myself right?

Am I trying to understand, or trying to control?

Am I seeking reconciliation, or revenge?

Am I acting from love, or from fear, pride, anger, or hurt?

Would I apply the same standard to myself?

Am I willing to discover that I am wrong?

The Key must be used in love.

Love is the intention and manner of the search; truth remains something we must actually test.

Twelve Tests

1. Text

What does the passage actually say?

Do not begin with what we think it says. Begin with the words that are actually there.

2. Context

What comes before and after the passage?

Who is speaking? Who is being addressed? What situation is being discussed?

3. Scripture Interprets Scripture

Are there genuine internal parallels?

A shared word, number, image, or theme does not automatically prove that two passages interpret one another.

Connections should be supported by context, repeated biblical usage, or other evidence.

4. Christ

Does the interpretation remain consistent with Jesus’ documented teaching and actions?

Our understanding of Christ must also be tested.

“Jesus would never…” should not become a shortcut that overrides Scripture.

5. Love

Does the interpretation preserve love of God and neighbor?

Love does not mean abandoning truth.

Truth does not give us permission to abandon love.

6. Truth

Are facts separated from assumptions?

Scripture says. Evidence shows. Interpretation suggests. Speculation explores.

Keep those categories visible.

7. Freedom

Does the interpretation avoid coercion?

The Key should never be used to force another person into belief, obedience, fear, dependence, or submission to an interpreter.

8. Humility

Can we admit:

“We don’t know.”

Understanding can grow.

Being willing to be corrected is part of seeking truth.

9. Consistency

Does the rule work in other passages too?

If we create a rule to explain one passage, test whether that same rule produces contradictions elsewhere.

10. No Forced Numbers

Are numerical patterns discovered rather than manufactured?

Numbers can be meaningful in Scripture, but numbers do not get their meaning from us.

Context gives them their role.

Interesting is not the same as true.

11. Fruit

What does the interpretation produce in practice?

Fruit is evidence to examine, not proof by itself.

Something feeling good does not automatically make it true.

Something being uncomfortable does not automatically make it false.

12. Return to Scripture

After all the analysis, return to the original text.

Does the interpretation still stand?

If it does not, let the interpretation go.

No Person Is Above the Key

No person becomes the Key.

No AI becomes the Key.

No organization becomes the Key.

No numerical pattern becomes the Key.

No interpretation becomes untouchable.

The messenger does not get a pass.

If I can use the Key to question you, you must be able to use the Key to question me.

A rule that cannot survive being applied to itself is a rule that needs examination.

The Key Must Be Able to Turn Back on Itself

The Key must test its own assumptions.

If one of its rules creates contradictions, fear, control, superiority, condemnation, or dependence on an interpreter, that rule must itself be examined.

The Key does not claim that every interpretation produced by it will be correct.

Instead, its purpose is to make it harder for an interpretation to become an unquestionable weapon of authority.

The Key Must Not Turn People Into the Enemy

Scripture says:

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood…”
— Ephesians 6:12

The surrounding passage directs attention toward truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation, the word of God, and prayer.

Therefore, the Key must never casually turn another human being into the enemy.

We can examine an action without dehumanizing the person.

We can examine an idea without attacking the person’s worth.

We can examine an influence without assuming that every person involved is the source of it.

Even that claim must be tested.

Correct the Idea Without Condemning the Person

We can say:

“I believe that idea is wrong.”

We can say:

“That action caused harm.”

We can say:

“I need a boundary here.”

Without saying:

“You are worthless.”

The goal is correction without hatred.

Truth without cruelty.

Boundaries without abandonment.

Freedom without coercion.

Truth and Love Must Remain Together

Jesus taught love of God and love of neighbor as central.

Truth matters.

Love matters.

Humility matters.

Freedom matters.

The Key should not separate them when Scripture holds them together.

Speak truth in love.

We Are Allowed to Discover Without Immediately Believing

A possibility can be explored without being declared true.

A pattern can be interesting without being authoritative.

A question can be valuable even when the answer is unknown.

We can say:

“This is something worth examining.”

Without saying:

“Therefore this must be true.”

This distinction protects curiosity without allowing curiosity to become certainty without evidence.

Simple Practice

When reading a difficult passage, ask:

1. What does the text actually say?
2. What is the context?
3. Is there a genuine biblical parallel?
4. What did Jesus actually teach or demonstrate that is relevant?
5. Does my interpretation preserve love?
6. What is fact, and what is assumption?
7. Does this preserve freedom?
8. Am I willing to be wrong?
9. Does the interpretation remain consistent elsewhere?
10. Am I forcing a number or pattern?
11. What fruit does this interpretation produce?
12. When I return to Scripture, does it still stand?

Then ask one more question:

Why am I looking for this answer?

What the Key Is Not

The Key is not a replacement for Scripture.

It is not a system for predicting the future.

It is not a method for identifying people as spiritual enemies.

It is not a way to establish one person as spiritually superior.

It is not a license to condemn people.

It is not a weapon.

It is not a substitute for prayer, wisdom, community, or responsible study.

It is not proof simply because a pattern appears.

The Purpose

The purpose of this Key is simple:

To help people seek truth without surrendering their freedom, pursue understanding without pride, correct error without hatred, and read Scripture without turning interpretation into a weapon.

The Key belongs to everyone.

No one stands above the Key.

Not the reader.

Not the creator.

Not the teacher.

Not the institution.

Not the technology.

Not AI.

Central Principle

Seek. Test. Listen. Examine. Love. Return to Scripture.

The goal was never to create another authority.

The goal is to help people seek truth, love God, love one another, and remain free to examine what they are taught.

A Key to Reading the Bible is a gift, not a weapon.
